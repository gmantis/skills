How the model works

REPLACE: the honest network finds the next block with probability p, the attacker with q = 1 − p. Being z blocks behind and needing to draw level is a gambler's-ruin walk: lines 1–6 solve its recursion and get (q/p)z, and the page checks that against the recursion solved by plain numbers (a 400-step tridiagonal system, no formula).

While the merchant waits for z honest blocks, the attacker makes some progress k. Exactly, k follows a negative binomial distribution; the paper assumes the honest blocks took exactly their average time, which makes k Poisson with mean λ = zq/p (line 8, the only approximation). Lines 9–10 average the catch-up chance over k. The "exact race" numbers use the negative binomial instead; a seeded simulation of 100 000 races checks them.

Each line's check evaluates both sides at the current q and z (or at a stated test value of r). Exact lines must agree to a relative difference below 10−9; the approximation line shows how far it is off, and counts as close within 10%.
