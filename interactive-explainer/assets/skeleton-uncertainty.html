How the model works

Every screened person is sick with probability prevalence. A sick person tests positive with probability sensitivity; a healthy person tests negative with probability specificity, so false alarms happen at 1 − specificity. Multiplying out gives four groups whose expected sizes out of 1,000 are the natural frequencies in the icon array. The chance that a positive is real is the true positives divided by all positives, which is Bayes' rule written with counts.

The simulation draws each of 1,000 people with a seeded random number generator, times. Its averages must agree with the exact numbers within about three standard errors (the validation table checks this live); its spread shows how far a single real town can land from the expectation.
