Analemma explorer: why Singapore's sunrise is earliest in November and latest in February

After Helmer Aslaksen (NUS), Which Day Does the Sun Rise Earliest in Singapore? Near the equator the day length hardly changes, yet sunrise drifts about 30 minutes over the year. That drift is the equation of time. Plot the Sun at the same clock time every morning and you get a figure-8, the rising analemma. Its axis is tilted to the horizon by an angle equal to your latitude. Whichever point of the figure-8 clears the horizon first gives the earliest sunrise. Move the sliders to watch latitude and the equation of time pull against each other. Use What if? to switch off Earth's uneven orbital speed or its axial tilt and see what the analemma would look like without each one.

Summary

Tug of war: latitude against the equation of time

How this is computed

Kepler orbit with eccentricity e. The mean anomaly is M = 360°·(d − perihelion)/365.2422, solved for the true anomaly ν. The longitude of perihelion is chosen so the March equinox (λ = 0) falls on day 79.4, about Mar 20. Sun's longitude λ = ν + ϖ, mean longitude L₀ = M + ϖ. Declination δ = asin(sin ε sin λ). Right ascension α = atan2(cos ε sin λ, cos λ). Equation of time EoT = 4·(L₀ − α) minutes. For Earth this agrees with the usual series formula (y·sin2L₀ − 2e·sinM + …) to within a few seconds.

Sunrise/sunset hour angle: cos H₀ = (sin h₀ − sin φ sin δ)/(cos φ cos δ), with h₀ = −0.833° or 0°. If |cos H₀| > 1 the Sun never rises (polar night) or never sets (polar day). Clock time = 12 h ∓ H₀/15 − EoT/60 + (UTC offset − longitude/15). Singapore (103.82° E, UTC+8) is +64.7 min, so its clocks run over an hour ahead of local solar time.

Sky view: the Sun's altitude/azimuth each day at one fixed clock time T, with hour angle H = 15°·(T − clock offset + EoT/60 − 12). The plot uses equal degree scales on both axes, so the angle between the analemma's axis and the horizon on screen is the real one. "Auto" puts T at the year's mean sunrise (or sunset) time, so the figure-8 straddles the horizon. The dashed axis is where the Sun would be with no equation of time (fixed hour angle, δ from −ε to +ε). Where it meets the horizon it's tilted by exactly the latitude. Days are whole days of a 365-day year evaluated at local noon, so dates can be off by about a day compared with an almanac.
