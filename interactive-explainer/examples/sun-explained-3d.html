Declination and the equation of time, in 3D

Two numbers describe where the Sun is at noon on any day. Declination says how far north or south it is. The equation of time says how far it is ahead of or behind the clock. Step through the explanation on the left. Drag either 3D view to rotate it and scroll to zoom. Click or drag on the charts to pick a day. For sunrise and sunset times, see the analemma explorer.

Orbit view: Earth going round the Sun

Not to scale: Earth and Sun are drawn far too big. The yellow line on the globe is the latitude where the Sun is straight overhead today, which is the declination.

Sky view: the sphere of sky around Earth

Earth at the centre, with the sky drawn as a sphere around it and seen from outside. The stars stay fixed; the Sun creeps around the ecliptic once a year.

Declination over the year

The Sun's latitude. Up and down once a year, between +ε and −ε. Only the tilt causes it.

Equation of time over the year

Sun ahead of the clock (+) or behind it (−). It is the sum of the tilt part (twice a year) and the speed part (once a year).

Where the equation of time comes from: each day's error

How much longer or shorter than 24 h each "Sun day" (noon to noon on a sundial) is. The equation of time is these seconds added up, day after day.

The analemma: the two numbers plotted against each other

Equation of time across and declination up. The width is stretched ×3 so the loops are easier to see; in the real sky the 8 is 3× narrower. The bright tail shows the last two months, so you can see which way the Sun travels round the 8.
